## Project overview

We are developing gravitational-wave models for extreme mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) in parametrized black-hole geometries. The goal is to study how departures from Kerr affect the accumulated waveform phase and assess whether LISA could distinguish these signals.

The calculation combines black-hole perturbation theory in the Newman–Penrose formalism with numerical solutions of Teukolsky-like equations. We use Sasaki–Nakamura transformations to make the radial equations better suited to numerical integration.

## Main steps and goals

1. **Calculate circular orbits:** Determine the secondary object's orbital energy, angular momentum, and frequency for each black-hole geometry.
2. **Solve the perturbation equations:** Construct homogeneous radial solutions with the appropriate horizon and infinity boundary conditions, using the Sasaki–Nakamura transformation.
3. **Include the orbiting source:** Build the sourced solution through a Green’s-function approach and calculate the gravitational-wave energy fluxes at infinity and the horizon.
4. **Evolve the inspiral:** Use energy balance to track the orbital radius and phase, then compare the accumulated gravitational-wave phase with a corresponding Kerr inspiral.
5. **Validate and extend the calculation:** Check numerical convergence and benchmark cases, explore different spins and deformation parameters, and assess the implications for LISA measurements.

## Current work

An initial braneworld benchmark provides a check of the framework. Ongoing work focuses on completing and validating the flux calculations, extending the study to additional black-hole geometries, and quantifying their effects on the inspiral waveform.

**Methods:** Black-hole perturbation theory, Newman–Penrose formalism, Teukolsky-like equations, Sasaki–Nakamura transformations, Green’s functions, and numerical inspiral modelling.
