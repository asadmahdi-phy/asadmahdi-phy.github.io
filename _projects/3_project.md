## Project overview

At **Nordita**, I am working with **Dr. Dhrubaditya Mitra** to investigate how spatially anisotropic quenched disorder affects the paramagnetic-to-ferromagnetic transition in the $\phi^4$ model. We aim to determine whether the disorder changes the universal critical behaviour and whether its directional dependence persists at large length scales.

The project combines analytical calculations in statistical field theory with numerical analysis of renormalization-group flows.

## Main steps and goals

1. **Formulate the disordered theory:** Introduce anisotropic quenched disorder into the model and examine its consequences at the mean-field level.
2. **Perform disorder averaging:** Use the replica method to obtain an effective theory and identify the interactions generated by the disorder.
3. **Derive the flow equations:** Evaluate the relevant Feynman diagrams and apply Wilsonian momentum-shell renormalization, including the rescaling of momenta and fields.
4. **Analyse fixed points and stability:** Solve the coupled flow equations numerically in Mathematica and use the eigenvalues of the linearized flow to identify relevant and irrelevant perturbations.
5. **Determine the critical behaviour:** Extract critical exponents where controlled fixed points are found and assess whether the disorder produces a distinct universality class or becomes irrelevant at large scales.

## Current work

My work focuses on calculating the diagrammatic corrections, deriving the coupled renormalization-group equations, and analysing their solutions. The numerical study follows trajectories from different initial conditions to examine fixed-point stability and the influence of disorder on the long-distance behaviour.

**Methods:** Statistical field theory, replica averaging, perturbative Feynman diagrams, Wilsonian renormalization, fixed-point analysis, and numerical differential equations in Mathematica.
